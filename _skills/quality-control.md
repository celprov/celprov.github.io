### Summary
Quality control was fundamental to ensure the reliability of neuroimaging analyses across all thesis projects. I developed and validated comprehensive QC protocols for functional and structural MRI, contributing open-source tools like MRIQC to the neuroimaging community. Educational initiatives helped spread QC knowledge, while continuous improvements to visualization tools enhanced the efficiency of manual quality assessments.

### Representative work
- **QC protocol development**: Established standardized protocols for multi-modal neuroimaging data inspection, including unprocessed and minimally preprocessed images, resulting in publications on QC best practices [Frontiers in Neuroimaging, 2023].
- **Educational talk on QC**: Presented advances in functional MRI QC methodology and their implications for reliable neuroimaging analysis at international conferences [Oral presentation, OHBM 2023].
- **'Crown to carpet plot' OSS contribution**: Enhanced fMRI visualization tools in MRIQC and fMRIPrep by incorporating novel QA/QC metrics for identifying motion and other artifacts, facilitating improved image quality assessment [Conference poster, ISMRM 2022].
- **Defacing bias investigation**: Quantified how facial feature removal affects human and automatic QC assessments of T1-weighted MRI, leading to recommendations for pre-defacing QA/QC procedures [Paper submitted, PLOS Biology].
