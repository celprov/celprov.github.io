### Summary
Machine learning techniques were employed throughout my thesis to enhance quality control, predictive modeling, and brain connectivity analyses. Particularly, I developed predictive models using XGBRegressor to assess motion artifacts in T1-weighted MRI scans, predicting signal-to-noise ratio degradation. Support Vector Machines were applied in clinical contexts for multimodal brain MRI analysis, while Bayesian modeling approaches characterized reliability in brain networks by incorporating prior knowledge and probability distributions for parameter estimation.

### Representative work
- **XGBRegressor for QC**: Developed gradient boosting models to predict head motion presence in structural MRI, contributing to automatic quality assessment protocols in neuroimaging pipelines [Poster, ISMRM 2024].
- **SVM on multimodal MRI**: Applied support vector machine classification for clinical diagnostics using multimodal brain MRI features, demonstrating improved predictive performance for neurological conditions [Thesis].
- **Bayesian modeling**: Implemented probabilistic models to characterize inter- and intra-scanner reliability in brain connectivity maps, enabling uncertainty quantification in neuroimaging analyses [Nature Methods Stage 1, 2023].
